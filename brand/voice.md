# Numena: Voice

We take complex human work seriously without sounding complicated to prove it. Numena is not a medical institution, not an AI company, and not a productivity tool. We sound human.

This document is the verbal identity. It follows from the [Foundation](foundation.md): rigor, restraint, and clinician authorship all show up in how we write.

## Principles

Four principles, in order of weight.

### 1. Be thoughtful

We believe good psychological work requires attention, interpretation, and judgment. Our language should reflect that.

We don't rush to conclusions. We make space for complexity without making things unnecessarily complicated.

- **Make room for nuance.** Avoid absolute claims when the evidence doesn't support them. Use language that reflects uncertainty honestly.
- **Choose precision over cleverness.** Specific words that describe what actually happens beat marketing gloss.
- **Let the reader think.** Don't explain every implication. Sometimes the strongest writing gives someone enough to make the connection themselves.

Short sentences help. So does a little silence.

### 2. Be human

Psychology is about people. Our language should never make their work feel like a data-processing problem.

We speak naturally, respectfully, and directly.

- **Speak one-to-one.** Use *you*, *your*, *we*, and *our*. Write as though speaking with a thoughtful professional, not presenting to an audience.
- **Use natural language.** Contractions are fine. Starting with *and*, *but*, or *so* is fine. If a simpler word works, use it.
- **Respect the person behind the work.** Never reduce psychologists to users, clinicians to workflows, or clients to data.

### 3. Be rigorous

We don't use confidence as a substitute for evidence. Our voice should communicate competence without pretending to know more than we do.

- **Say what we know.** Be specific about what the product does and doesn't do.
- **Don't hide uncertainty.** *Might*, *possible*, *uncertain*, and *we don't know yet* aren't weaknesses when they're accurate. They're part of responsible reasoning.
- **Explain the reasoning.** Whenever possible, show the relationship between an insight and the information behind it.

### 4. Be quietly confident

We know what we believe in. We don't need to shout about it. Assured rather than authoritative; intelligent rather than academic; serious without becoming austere.

- **Make a clear statement.** Don't hide behind vague marketing language.
- **Avoid hype.** No grand claims about transforming healthcare, revolutionizing psychology, or replacing professional judgment.
- **Use restraint.** No manufactured certainty, no slogans, no overclaiming. The strongest line is the one a reader can act on — and the confidence should come from the idea itself.

## Prefer this over that

| Not this | This | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Understand your clients better with AI." | "See patterns across your work, while keeping the context behind them in view." | Say what the product actually does; leave room for nuance. |
| "Unlock deeper clinical insights." | "Trace a hypothesis back to the observations that informed it." | The first tells you something; the second only sounds like marketing. |

More before/after pairs:

- Prefer → "Keep the context behind each observation visible." — over *Centralize client data for better decision-making.* The difference is small in words, significant in attitude.
- Say: "The system helps you organize observations, connect evidence, and examine competing hypotheses." Not: "AI understands your clients and helps you make better diagnoses."
- A truthful uncertainty reads stronger than manufactured certainty: "This pattern appears in three sessions. It's worth investigating, but it's not enough to establish an explanation."
- Show the path: "See which observations support a pattern, which contradict it, and what information is still missing."
- Restraint over the grand claim: "Less time reconstructing your reasoning. More time thinking about it."

## Corporate phrases we avoid

`leverage` · `optimize workflows` · `unlock insights` · `drive efficiency` · `empower practitioners` · `next-generation` · `AI-powered`

## A simple test, before publishing

| Question | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Is it clear? | Could someone understand it without knowing our terminology? |
| Is it honest? | Are we claiming more than we can support? |
| Is it human? | Would a psychologist actually say this? |
| Is it useful? | Does the reader learn something, or understand what the product does? |
| Is it ours? | Could any generic AI startup say the same thing? If yes, rewrite it. |
