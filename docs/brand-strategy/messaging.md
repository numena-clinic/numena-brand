# Messaging and Verbal Identity

## Purpose

This document defines what Numena communicates and how it communicates.

**Messaging** explains the product, its intended value, and its main messages.

**Verbal identity** defines the voice, tone, and writing principles used to express those messages consistently.

Messaging should reflect the positioning in `positioning.md` and the beliefs in `brand-foundation.md`. Product claims must remain consistent with demonstrated capabilities.

# Part I. Messaging

## 1. Product description

**Canonical description**

Numena is a professional practice platform for psychologists working with complex clinical information.

**Expanded description**

Numena aims to help psychologists organize, examine, and revisit clinical information while preserving the context and professional judgment involved in understanding it.

Use the canonical description when a brief introduction is sufficient. Use the expanded description when the audience needs to understand the intended value.

The expanded description should be adjusted if the product's actual capabilities do not yet support these activities.

## 2. Value proposition

**Working value proposition**

A more structured way for psychologists to work through complex clinical information without giving up the nuance or judgment their work requires.

The value proposition connects three elements:

- **Audience:** Psychologists working with complex clinical information.
- **Need:** A way to organize and examine information as understanding develops.
- **Intended value:** Greater clarity and continuity without replacing professional judgment.

This is a strategic hypothesis. Research and product evaluation should establish whether the need is significant and whether Numena delivers the intended value.

## 3. Core message

**Make complex clinical thinking easier to see.**

Numena aims to help psychologists examine information, consider relationships, and revisit interpretations as their understanding develops.

The message should communicate the product's intended purpose without suggesting that it determines what information means or guarantees better clinical decisions.

**Brand expression:** *Better thinking, made visible.*

The core message explains the intended product value. The brand expression communicates the broader idea behind Numena. They serve different purposes and should not be used interchangeably in every context.

## 4. Supporting messages

Use these messages selectively according to the audience's question or context. They are not a checklist that every communication must include.

### Context matters

Help psychologists consider information in relation to other relevant observations and interpretations.

### Interpretations can evolve

Support the examination and revision of hypotheses as new information becomes available.

### Distinguish information from interpretation

Make it easier to examine what was observed, what was inferred, and what remains uncertain, where the product supports these distinctions.

### Professional judgment remains central

Numena is intended to support psychologists' reasoning, not replace their responsibility for interpretation and decisions.

### Technology serves the work

Explain technology through the professional need it addresses, not through novelty or claims of intelligence alone.

These messages describe the intended direction of the product. Use them as concrete product claims only when the relevant capabilities are available and demonstrable.

## 5. Elevator pitch

### Short

Numena is a professional practice platform for psychologists working with complex clinical information.

### Conversational

Psychologists work with information that accumulates and changes over time. Numena aims to help them organize that information, examine relationships, and revisit interpretations without giving up professional judgment.

### Extended

Understanding complex clinical information takes more than recording it. Observations, patterns, and interpretations develop over time, and their meaning can change as new information emerges.

Numena is being developed as a professional practice platform to help psychologists work with this complexity. Its intended focus is on making information easier to organize, examine, and revisit while keeping professional judgment central.

Use the short version for introductions, the conversational version for informal explanations, and the extended version when more context is needed. The longer versions must be updated to reflect the product's actual stage and capabilities.

## 6. Messaging hierarchy

When explaining Numena, prioritize information in this order:

1. **What it is:** A professional practice platform for psychologists.
2. **What it helps with:** Organizing and examining complex clinical information.
3. **Why it matters:** Context and interpretations develop over time.
4. **What guides it:** Professional judgment, nuance, and openness to revision.
5. **What it stands for:** Better thinking, made visible.

Start with concrete meaning. Use the broader brand idea to deepen understanding, not to substitute for a clear product explanation.

# Part II. Verbal Identity

## 7. Voice

Numena's voice should remain consistent across contexts.

| Quality | How it sounds | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Thoughtful | Considers context and implications. | Abstract or unnecessarily elaborate language. |
| Clear | Explains ideas directly. | Jargon, ambiguity, and oversimplification. |
| Calm | Measured and composed. | Hype, pressure, or alarmism. |
| Respectful | Recognizes the reader's expertise and autonomy. | Patronizing explanations or prescriptive authority. |
| Discerning | Distinguishes facts, interpretations, and uncertainty. | Unsupported certainty or claims of objectivity. |

The voice should communicate substance without trying to sound more intelligent, human, or authoritative than necessary.

## 8. Tone by context

The voice stays consistent; tone changes with the situation and its stakes.

| Context | Tone | Writing approach |
|---|---|---|
| Product explanation | Direct and informative | Explain what the product does and whom it serves. |
| Instructions | Clear and practical | Give the next action without unnecessary explanation. |
| Complex concepts | Patient and precise | Explain terminology and preserve relevant nuance. |
| Sensitive topics | Respectful and measured | Avoid assumptions, casual language, and false reassurance. |
| Uncertainty or limitations | Honest and specific | State what is known, what is uncertain, and what remains to be checked. |
| Errors | Calm and accountable | Explain what happened and what the user can do next. |
| Marketing | Specific and restrained | Lead with a real need and substantiated value. |
| Onboarding | Supportive and purposeful | Explain why a step matters without pressuring the user. |
| AI-generated content | Transparent and appropriately qualified | Make the status and limitations of suggestions clear where relevant. |

Tone should reflect the consequences of the communication. Higher-stakes situations require greater precision, transparency, and care.

## 9. Writing principles

1. **Lead with meaning.** Explain the point before the philosophy behind it.
2. **Use plain language.** Prefer familiar words unless a technical term adds precision.
3. **Be precise, not exhaustive.** Include what the reader needs to understand or act.
4. **Preserve relevant nuance.** Simplify the explanation, not the underlying reality.
5. **Distinguish knowledge from inference.** Do not present an interpretation or possibility as an established fact.
6. **Explain rather than persuade.** Give readers enough context to make their own judgments.
7. **Substantiate claims.** Describe actual capabilities and demonstrated benefits.
8. **Respect the reader's expertise.** Do not overexplain familiar concepts or dictate conclusions.
9. **Use confidence carefully.** Be direct when evidence is strong and explicit when it is not.
10. **Avoid artificial personhood.** Be considerate without implying human feelings, understanding, or relationships the product does not possess.

## 10. Vocabulary

### Preferred language

Use these words when they accurately describe the task or capability:

| Word | Use |
|---|---|
| Observe | Describe information that has been noticed or recorded. |
| Examine | Investigate information or a possible relationship. |
| Explore | Consider possibilities without implying a conclusion. |
| Evidence | Identify information relevant to evaluating a claim or hypothesis. |
| Hypothesis | Describe a possible explanation that remains open to examination. |
| Interpretation | Identify meaning assigned to information. |
| Context | Explain the circumstances or relationships relevant to understanding something. |
| Pattern | Describe a recurring relationship or feature, without automatically implying significance. |
| Uncertainty | Acknowledge what remains unknown or unresolved. |
| Reflect | Consider information and its implications. |
| Revisit | Return to an earlier interpretation or decision. |
| Revise | Update an interpretation when warranted. |
| Judgment | Refer to the professional evaluation and decisions that remain the user's responsibility. |

These are preferred terms, not mandatory keywords. Choose the clearest word for the situation.

### Avoid or scrutinize

Avoid language that implies unsupported performance, certainty, or authority.

- **Hype:** “Revolutionary,” “game-changing,” “cutting-edge.”
- **Vague transformation:** “Unlock potential,” “transform practice,” “optimize everything.”
- **Unsupported certainty:** “Discover the truth,” “eliminate uncertainty,” “guaranteed clarity.”
- **Unwarranted clinical authority:** “AI clinical expert,” “diagnose with confidence,” “objective interpretation.”
- **Empty technology claims:** “AI-powered intelligence,” “intelligent companion,” “never miss an insight.”
- **Unsubstantiated ease:** “Effortless understanding,” “seamless experience.”

Do not treat this list as a mechanical blacklist. A term may be appropriate in a specific, substantiated context; the underlying problem is language that obscures meaning or promises more than the product can deliver.

## 11. Examples

These examples illustrate the intended writing approach. Product-specific examples should only be used if the relevant functionality exists.

### Product description

Avoid: “Revolutionizing the future of psychological practice.”

Prefer: “A professional practice platform for psychologists working with complex clinical information.”

Why: States the product category and audience directly.

### Interpreting information

Avoid: “Discover the true meaning behind every observation.”

Prefer: “Examine observations and consider possible interpretations.”

Why: Supports exploration without promising a single definitive meaning.

### Organizing information

Avoid: “Never lose an important insight again.”

Prefer: “Keep relevant information connected so it can be revisited in context.”

Why: Describes an intended benefit without guaranteeing completeness.

### AI capabilities

Avoid: “Your intelligent clinical companion knows what matters.”

Prefer: “Review suggestions as possible interpretations, and evaluate them against the available information.”

Why: Avoids implying human understanding or independent clinical authority. Use only where the product actually provides suggestions.

### Uncertainty

Avoid: “Numena gives you confidence in every decision.”

Prefer: “Some questions remain unresolved. Keep track of what is known and what needs further examination.”

Why: Communicates uncertainty without treating it as a failure.

### Marketing

Avoid: “Transform your practice with effortless AI.”

Prefer: “A more structured way to examine complex clinical information.”

Why: Leads with the intended professional value rather than technology hype.

## 12. Applying verbal identity

- **Website:** Explain what Numena is and whom it serves before introducing abstract brand language.
- **Product interface:** Prefer concise, actionable language that helps users understand what they can do.
- **Onboarding:** Explain the purpose of a step and avoid unnecessary pressure.
- **Notifications and errors:** Be specific, calm, and accountable.
- **AI-generated content:** Distinguish generated suggestions from source information and professional conclusions wherever applicable.
- **Marketing and educational content:** Separate product facts, intended benefits, hypotheses, and broader points of view.

## 13. Review checklist

Before publishing a message, ask:

- Is it clear what Numena is or what the reader needs to know?
- Does it address a relevant audience need?
- Are product claims supported by actual capabilities?
- Does it distinguish evidence from interpretation where necessary?
- Does it respect uncertainty and professional judgment?
- Is the language specific, plain, and appropriate to the context?
- Does it avoid unnecessary hype or implied authority?

If the message fails these checks, revise it before publication.
