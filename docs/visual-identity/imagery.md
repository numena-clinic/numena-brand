# Imagery

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the principles for selecting, creating, and using imagery across Numena’s brand communications.

It establishes how photography, illustration, and abstract visuals should represent the brand, its audience, and the subject of psychological practice.

The goal is to create a recognizable and coherent visual direction while ensuring that imagery remains credible, respectful, and appropriate to its context.

This document defines the strategic direction for imagery. It does not establish technical image specifications, asset libraries, or product interface requirements.

## 2. Creative Direction

Numena’s imagery should communicate **human complexity, thoughtful understanding, and clarity without oversimplification**.

Psychology concerns people's experiences, relationships, behavior, and interpretations of the world. Its visual representation should respect that complexity rather than reduce it to familiar symbols or exaggerated emotional expressions.

Imagery should balance three qualities:

- **Human:** grounded in real experiences, relationships, and individual differences.
- **Thoughtful:** considered, nuanced, and appropriate to the subject.
- **Clear:** visually coherent, purposeful, and easy to interpret.

The result should feel contemporary and approachable without appearing superficial, overly clinical, or disconnected from psychological practice.

## 3. Core Principles

### 3.1 Represent people with dignity

People should be represented as individuals, not as examples of a diagnosis, emotional state, or psychological problem.

Avoid imagery that portrays people as helpless, broken, dangerous, or defined by their difficulties.

Prefer representations that preserve agency, individuality, and the complexity of human experience.

### 3.2 Authenticity over performance

Imagery should feel credible rather than staged to communicate an obvious emotional message.

Avoid exaggerated expressions, artificial interactions, and stock photography that presents happiness, distress, or personal transformation as universal experiences.

Authenticity does not require every image to be documentary photography. It requires the visual representation to feel plausible and appropriate to its purpose.

### 3.3 Nuance over cliché

Psychological experiences cannot always be represented through a single expression, gesture, or symbol.

Avoid relying on familiar visual shortcuts such as puzzle pieces for the mind, brains for intelligence, tangled lines for distress, or isolated figures for loneliness.

These elements are not universally inappropriate, but they should not become default substitutes for meaningful visual direction.

### 3.4 Clarity over literalness

Imagery does not always need to depict its subject directly.

Abstract visuals, carefully chosen environments, or visual metaphors may communicate ideas that are difficult to photograph.

The connection between the image and its intended message should nevertheless be understandable within its context.

### 3.5 Consistency without uniformity

Images should share a coherent visual character without making every communication look identical.

Consistency may come from subject selection, composition, lighting, tonal treatment, illustration style, or the relationship between imagery and typography.

The appropriate combination depends on the medium and communication objective.

## 4. Photography

Photography should present people, environments, and interactions in ways that feel credible, respectful, and relevant to Numena.

### Subject matter

Appropriate subjects may include:

- People in everyday environments.
- Thoughtful, natural interactions.
- Professional environments related to psychological practice.
- Details of spaces and objects that establish context.
- Moments of reflection, conversation, and ordinary human experience.

These are possible directions, not a requirement that every image depict a person or a therapy session.

### People and expressions

Prefer natural expressions and believable body language over exaggerated emotional performances.

Avoid implying that a particular facial expression reliably reveals someone's internal state or psychological condition.

Do not use imagery to imply that a person has a particular diagnosis, needs treatment, or has achieved a psychological outcome unless the context supports that representation.

### Environments

Environments should feel plausible and appropriate to the intended audience.

Clinical environments may be used when they provide relevant context, but the brand should not rely exclusively on sterile offices, examination rooms, or institutional settings.

Likewise, avoid making every psychological interaction appear in an idealized, luxurious, or excessively curated environment.

### Composition and lighting

Prefer considered compositions with a clear focal point and enough visual space for the image to function in its intended format.

Lighting and color treatment should support the subject without creating an artificial emotional atmosphere.

Avoid excessive retouching, heavy filters, and treatments that make different images feel disconnected.

## 5. Illustration

Illustration may be used when photography cannot explain an idea clearly, when a concept is inherently abstract, or when a more controlled visual representation is appropriate.

### Purpose

Illustrations may help to:

- Explain relationships between ideas.
- Represent abstract or complex concepts.
- Add visual interest to editorial content.
- Support educational material.
- Create original brand imagery where photography would feel generic or misleading.

### Style

Illustration should follow Numena’s broader graphic language: clarity, purposeful form, restraint, and a balance between human qualities and visual structure.

A consistent illustration direction should be established before multiple styles are introduced.

Avoid mixing unrelated illustration styles, decorative characters, generic corporate illustrations, and realistic imagery without a clear reason.

### Representation

Illustrations should avoid simplistic visual associations between appearance and psychological state.

Do not use color, shape, posture, or facial features as though they provide a universal visual language for specific emotions or diagnoses.

When illustrating psychological concepts, prioritize conceptual clarity over visual novelty.

## 6. Abstract and Conceptual Imagery

Abstract imagery may be appropriate for expressing ideas that do not have a direct photographic equivalent.

Possible directions include:

- Relationships between distinct elements.
- Layers, boundaries, and connections.
- Patterns that suggest variation or complexity.
- Geometric and organic forms in deliberate tension.
- Visual structures that suggest reflection, change, or organization.

These directions are exploratory rather than mandatory brand motifs.

Abstract imagery should have a clear role in the composition. It should not be used merely to make an asset appear more sophisticated, scientific, or technologically advanced.

Avoid visuals that imply unsupported claims about the brain, cognition, therapeutic outcomes, or psychological processes.

## 7. Imagery and Color

Imagery should work coherently with Numena’s established brand color palette.

Color may come from the image itself, from deliberate art direction, or from graphic elements surrounding the image.

The palette does not need to appear in every photograph. Forcing brand colors into imagery can reduce authenticity and create unnecessary visual uniformity.

When color grading or illustration uses the brand palette:

- Preserve sufficient contrast and visual hierarchy.
- Avoid excessive saturation or effects that compete with the subject.
- Maintain natural and credible skin tones in photography.
- Use accent colors selectively.
- Evaluate the image in its intended composition rather than in isolation.

Refer to the Brand Color Strategy for the palette and its intended roles.

## 8. Representation and Ethical Considerations

Imagery involving psychology requires particular care because visual choices can influence how people understand themselves and others.

### Avoid stigma

Do not associate psychological difficulties with threatening, unstable, weak, or inherently damaged people.

Avoid visual narratives that frame individuals as problems to be fixed.

### Avoid unsupported interpretation

An image cannot establish a diagnosis, explain the cause of a behavior, or demonstrate a therapeutic outcome by itself.

Do not use imagery in ways that imply otherwise.

### Respect diversity

Represent people across different ages, appearances, backgrounds, abilities, relationships, and life circumstances where relevant to the communication objective.

Diversity should be credible and contextual rather than tokenistic.

No single image or campaign needs to represent everyone. The broader collection of imagery should avoid consistently privileging one narrow representation of psychological experience.

### Protect privacy and consent

When using identifiable people, real clinical environments, or material connected to psychological practice, ensure that appropriate permissions and consent are in place.

Do not expose identifiable client information or use clinical material in ways that compromise confidentiality.

For synthetic or AI-generated imagery, do not present fabricated people, scenes, or outcomes as authentic clinical evidence or real client experiences.

### Avoid misleading emotional narratives

Do not suggest that therapy always produces visible happiness, that psychological change follows a simple progression, or that distress can be reliably recognized from appearance.

Imagery should leave room for the diversity and complexity of human experience.

## 9. Image Selection and Creation

Every image should be selected or created for a defined purpose.

Before approving an image, consider:

1. **Relevance:** Does it support the intended message?
2. **Credibility:** Does it feel plausible and appropriate to the context?
3. **Sensitivity:** Does it represent people and psychological experiences respectfully?
4. **Clarity:** Is the subject or visual idea understandable?
5. **Consistency:** Does it fit Numena’s visual direction?
6. **Composition:** Does it work in the intended format and alongside other content?
7. **Accessibility:** Can the essential meaning be communicated to people who cannot perceive the image?
8. **Rights and consent:** Are usage rights, permissions, and applicable privacy requirements satisfied?

An image should not be selected solely because it is attractive, fashionable, or visually consistent with the palette.

Its contribution to the communication objective matters more than its decorative appeal.

## 10. What to Avoid

Numena’s imagery should generally avoid:

- Generic stock photography of people smiling at laptops.
- Exaggerated emotional expressions used to communicate psychological states.
- Puzzle pieces, brains, and other overused psychological symbols without a specific rationale.
- Visual clichés that equate distress with darkness, isolation, or broken objects.
- Idealized therapy scenes that misrepresent professional practice.
- Overly clinical imagery that makes the brand feel institutional.
- Artificially perfect people and environments that undermine credibility.
- Inconsistent illustration styles.
- Decorative abstract imagery without a meaningful connection to the subject.
- Images that imply diagnoses, scientific findings, or therapeutic outcomes without evidence.
- Representations that stigmatize or reduce people to a single characteristic.

These guidelines are intended to encourage thoughtful decisions, not to prohibit entire image categories regardless of context.

## 11. Application Across Touchpoints

The role of imagery should adapt to the purpose of each medium.

- **Website:** establish the brand’s character, support positioning, and help visitors understand the product and its audience.
- **Educational content:** support explanation without replacing evidence or substantive information.
- **Editorial content:** complement the subject while respecting the complexity of the topic.
- **Presentations and reports:** clarify context and relationships without distracting from the content.
- **Campaigns:** allow stronger creative expression while preserving credibility and sensitivity.
- **Product experiences:** use imagery only when it supports comprehension, orientation, or a meaningful part of the experience.

These are strategic guidelines. Specific image dimensions, cropping rules, file formats, and interface behavior should be documented separately where needed.

## 12. Guiding Statement

**Numena’s imagery should represent human experience with clarity, credibility, and respect.**

It should make complex subjects more approachable without reducing them to clichés, and communicate warmth without sacrificing nuance or trust.

## 13. Relationship to Other Documents

This document works alongside the other parts of Numena’s brand documentation.

- **Brand Foundation:** defines the brand’s purpose, positioning, audience, and strategic direction.
- **Brand Color Strategy:** defines the palette and its intended roles.
- **Graphic Language:** establishes the broader principles governing shapes, lines, patterns, composition, and visual expression.
- **Visual Identity:** documents the identity elements and their specific visual direction.
- **Design System (future):** defines implementation-ready rules for imagery and other visual assets used within the product interface.

Imagery is one part of Numena’s visual identity. Its purpose is to strengthen the brand’s communication, not to replace its strategy or become a disconnected decorative layer.
