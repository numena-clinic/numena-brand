# Layout

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the principles governing layout and composition across Numena’s brand communications.

It establishes how content, typography, imagery, color, and graphic elements should be organized to create clear, coherent, and recognizable visual experiences.

The goal is to make information easier to understand while preserving the balance between human complexity and structured thinking that defines Numena’s visual direction.

This document establishes brand-level principles. It does not define interface grids, spacing tokens, responsive breakpoints, or component specifications. Those belong in the future design system where applicable.

## 2. Creative Direction

Numena’s layouts should communicate **clarity through structure, with enough flexibility to accommodate complexity**.

The composition should help people understand what matters, how information relates, and where to direct their attention.

Layouts should balance three qualities:

- **Clarity:** information is easy to scan, navigate, and understand.
- **Structure:** relationships between elements are deliberate and consistent.
- **Flexibility:** compositions adapt to different content, contexts, and formats without losing their coherence.

The result should feel organized without becoming rigid, expressive without becoming chaotic, and spacious without wasting attention.

## 3. Core Principles

### 3.1 Hierarchy before decoration

Every composition should have a clear visual hierarchy.

The most important information should receive the greatest visual emphasis, followed by supporting content and secondary details.

Hierarchy may be established through scale, position, contrast, typography, spacing, or imagery.

Avoid giving every element equal visual weight. When everything competes for attention, nothing stands out.

### 3.2 Structure that supports understanding

Elements should be arranged according to their meaning and relationship to one another.

Related information should feel connected. Distinct topics should remain distinguishable. Supporting elements should reinforce the main message rather than compete with it.

Structure should emerge from the content's purpose, not from an arbitrary preference for symmetry or uniformity.

### 3.3 Space as an active element

Empty space is part of the composition. It separates ideas, creates emphasis, improves readability, and gives important elements room to stand out.

Use space deliberately to establish relationships and distinguish levels of information.

Avoid both excessive density and unnecessary emptiness. The appropriate balance depends on the content, medium, and communication objective.

### 3.4 Consistency without repetition

Layouts should share recognizable principles without relying on the same composition for every asset.

Recurring approaches to hierarchy, alignment, spacing, and visual emphasis create coherence. Variations in scale, balance, and arrangement allow individual communications to respond to their specific purpose.

Consistency should come from shared logic, not identical templates.

### 3.5 Simplicity without oversimplification

Complex information often benefits from clear grouping, progressive disclosure, and carefully separated levels of detail.

However, simplifying a layout should not remove distinctions that matter to the message.

The objective is to make complexity easier to navigate, not to pretend that it does not exist.

## 4. Visual Hierarchy

Visual hierarchy determines the order in which people are likely to notice and interpret information.

Numena’s compositions should make this order intentional.

### Primary information

The primary element communicates the main message, subject, or purpose of the asset.

It may be a headline, key statement, image, illustration, or central piece of information.

Its prominence should reflect its actual importance rather than an arbitrary desire to make the composition visually dramatic.

### Supporting information

Supporting elements provide context, explanation, or evidence for the primary message.

They should be visually connected to the element they support while remaining distinguishable from it.

### Secondary information

Captions, metadata, references, annotations, and other supplementary details should remain accessible without competing unnecessarily with the main content.

Their reduced prominence should not compromise legibility or accessibility.

### Establishing hierarchy

Hierarchy can be created through:

- Differences in size and typographic weight.
- Position and alignment.
- Contrast between foreground and background.
- Grouping and separation.
- The placement and scale of imagery.
- The use of color for selective emphasis.

Use a combination of techniques appropriate to the composition. Do not rely on color alone to communicate importance or relationships.

## 5. Alignment and Organization

Alignment establishes order and helps people recognize relationships between elements.

Numena’s layouts should use deliberate alignment to create visual continuity and reduce unnecessary cognitive effort.

### Alignment

Align elements when they share a meaningful relationship, such as a common heading, content group, or visual boundary.

Intentional misalignment may create emphasis or contrast, but should be clearly purposeful.

Avoid inconsistent alignment that makes unrelated elements appear connected or makes related elements difficult to associate.

### Grouping

Elements that belong together should be visually grouped through proximity, shared alignment, background treatment, or other appropriate cues.

Distinct groups should have enough separation to make their boundaries understandable.

Grouping should reflect the structure of the information rather than simply filling available space.

### Balance

Compositions should distribute visual weight deliberately.

Symmetrical layouts can communicate stability and order. Asymmetrical layouts can introduce movement, contrast, and emphasis.

Neither approach is inherently preferable. Choose according to the purpose and character of the communication.

## 6. Spacing and Density

Spacing determines how elements relate and how much information a composition can comfortably accommodate.

Numena should use spacing to establish hierarchy, separate concepts, and support reading.

### Separation

Use greater separation between distinct topics and closer spacing between elements that belong together.

Spacing should communicate relationships consistently within each composition.

### Density

Information density should reflect the task and audience.

Editorial content may require sustained reading and relatively restrained visual interruption. Presentations may need stronger separation and fewer competing elements. Educational material may require additional grouping to make complex subjects easier to follow.

Dense layouts are not inherently bad, and spacious layouts are not inherently good. Their effectiveness depends on whether the arrangement supports the intended use.

### Restraint

Avoid compressing elements simply to fit more content into a limited area.

Also avoid excessive spacing that disconnects related information or makes the composition feel fragmented.

Specific spacing values should be established within the relevant visual identity or design-system specifications rather than invented independently for every asset.

## 7. Composition and Rhythm

Composition determines how elements interact across the visual field.

Numena’s layouts should use variation and repetition to guide attention while maintaining a coherent overall structure.

### Scale and proportion

Differences in scale can establish emphasis and create visual interest.

Use proportion to clarify the relationship between elements rather than introducing dramatic differences without a communicative purpose.

### Rhythm

Repetition, spacing, alignment, and changes in scale can establish visual rhythm.

A consistent rhythm makes a composition easier to follow. Controlled variation prevents it from becoming monotonous.

### Asymmetry and contrast

Asymmetry can create a more dynamic composition, particularly when balancing text with imagery or contrasting different levels of information.

Contrast should remain purposeful. Avoid introducing visual tension that distracts from the content or makes the reading order unclear.

### Direction and movement

The arrangement of elements can suggest where attention should move next.

Use position, alignment, visual connections, and hierarchy to create a natural progression through the content.

Do not assume that every audience will follow a single predetermined reading path. Important information should remain understandable even when people scan rather than read sequentially.

## 8. Typography, Color, and Imagery

Layout brings Numena’s visual identity elements together into a coherent composition.

### Typography

Typography establishes hierarchy, reading order, and tone.

Headings, body text, supporting details, and captions should have distinguishable roles. Their arrangement should support comprehension rather than rely on stylistic variation alone.

Refer to the Visual Identity documentation for Numena’s typographic direction.

### Color

Color can distinguish sections, emphasize selected information, and establish visual relationships.

Use the established brand palette consistently with its defined hierarchy. Color should support the composition rather than become the primary source of structure.

Refer to the Brand Color Strategy for color roles and usage principles.

### Imagery

Images should be positioned according to their contribution to the message.

They may establish context, support an explanation, introduce a visual focal point, or create a deliberate contrast with surrounding content.

Avoid placing imagery merely to fill an empty area. Consider its relationship to nearby text, its visual weight, and the space required for both elements to function.

Refer to the Imagery documentation for image selection and representation principles.

### Graphic elements

Lines, shapes, patterns, and other graphic elements may divide sections, connect related ideas, or establish rhythm.

Their placement should reinforce the information hierarchy. Decorative elements should not obscure content or create ambiguity about what belongs together.

Refer to the Graphic Language documentation for the principles governing these elements.

## 9. Layout Across Touchpoints

The same principles should adapt to different communication contexts.

### Website and marketing pages

Prioritize clear messaging, recognizable hierarchy, and a deliberate progression from the main proposition to supporting information.

Use space and composition to make key information easy to identify without making every section visually dominant.

### Editorial and educational content

Prioritize reading comfort, logical grouping, and clear distinctions between explanations, examples, references, and supporting material.

Avoid excessive visual interruptions that compete with sustained attention.

### Presentations

Use a clear focal point and a limited number of competing elements.

Each slide or page should have a discernible purpose. Complex information may require multiple stages of explanation rather than being compressed into a single composition.

### Reports and documents

Support sustained reading through consistent hierarchy, predictable alignment, and clear relationships between headings, paragraphs, figures, and references.

Complex material should remain navigable without removing meaningful qualifications or distinctions.

### Campaigns and brand communications

Allow greater variation, expressive composition, and stronger visual contrast where these choices serve the message.

Creative expression should remain consistent with Numena’s broader visual identity.

### Product experiences

In the product, layout must prioritize usability, comprehension, and the user's task.

Brand expression should support these goals rather than compete with them. Product-specific rules for responsive behavior, navigation, content density, and component arrangement should be defined in the future design system.

## 10. Adaptability and Accessibility

Layouts must work within the constraints of their intended medium.

A composition that works in a large presentation may not translate directly to a narrow mobile screen, a printed document, or a small social media graphic.

Adapt the arrangement when necessary rather than shrinking every element proportionally.

Consider:

- Whether the hierarchy remains clear at different sizes.
- Whether text remains legible.
- Whether content order remains logical.
- Whether important information is distinguishable without relying on color alone.
- Whether sufficient space remains for interactive controls in digital contexts.
- Whether the composition accommodates longer text and different content lengths.
- Whether the layout remains usable when zoomed or viewed with assistive technologies.

Accessibility should be considered from the beginning of the design process, not added after the composition is complete.

## 11. What to Avoid

Numena’s layouts should generally avoid:

- Compositions in which every element competes for attention.
- Decorative symmetry that does not support the content.
- Inconsistent alignment without a clear rationale.
- Arbitrary spacing that obscures relationships.
- Excessive density that makes information difficult to scan.
- Excessive emptiness that disconnects related content.
- Overuse of cards, boxes, dividers, or containers.
- Repeated templates that make every communication feel identical.
- Unnecessary visual effects that weaken hierarchy.
- Layouts that depend entirely on color to communicate distinctions.
- Compositions that prioritize brand expression over comprehension.
- Fixed arrangements that fail when content or format changes.

These principles guide judgment rather than prescribe one universal layout.

## 12. Evaluation Criteria

Before approving a composition, consider:

1. **Hierarchy:** Is the most important information immediately identifiable?
2. **Clarity:** Can people understand how the content is organized?
3. **Relationships:** Are related elements grouped and distinct elements separated?
4. **Balance:** Is visual weight distributed intentionally?
5. **Readability:** Does the arrangement support the intended level of reading and scanning?
6. **Restraint:** Does each prominent element contribute to the message?
7. **Consistency:** Does the composition align with Numena’s broader visual direction?
8. **Adaptability:** Can the layout accommodate its intended formats and content variations?
9. **Accessibility:** Does the hierarchy remain perceivable and usable under different viewing conditions?
10. **Purpose:** Does the layout make the intended communication easier to understand?

The criteria should be evaluated in context. The best composition is the one that serves the communication objective, not necessarily the one that looks the most minimal or visually striking.

## 13. Relationship to Other Documents

This document defines the principles governing how visual elements are organized within Numena’s communications.

It works alongside the other brand documents:

- **Brand Foundation:** defines the brand’s purpose, positioning, audience, and strategic direction.
- **Brand Color Strategy:** defines the color palette, hierarchy, and intended use.
- **Graphic Language:** defines the principles behind shapes, lines, patterns, and other graphic elements.
- **Imagery:** defines the selection, creation, and responsible use of photography, illustration, and abstract visuals.
- **Visual Identity:** defines the visual identity elements and their specific direction.
- **Design System (future):** translates relevant product-layout decisions into implementation-ready rules for grids, spacing, responsive behavior, and components.

Layout connects these elements into a coherent visual experience. Its purpose is to make communication clearer while preserving the flexibility required by different content and contexts.

## 14. Guiding Statement

**Numena’s layouts create order that helps people understand complexity.**

They should establish clear hierarchy without unnecessary rigidity, use space with intention, and organize information without stripping away meaningful nuance.
